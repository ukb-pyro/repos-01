Executive Summary

The Problem: Cognitive Overload at the Frontlines

Nonclinical youth mental health workers — peer supporters, school counselors, community health workers — are the first line of defense for millions of underserved young people. They face an impossible task: navigate complex, emotionally charged, high-stakes scenarios without real-time guidance, without structured decision frameworks, and without adequate institutional support.

The result is predictable: decision fatigue, burnout, and high turnover. The very workforce that mental health systems depend on to reach vulnerable youth is itself becoming a casualty of the same systemic neglect it is trying to address.

No technical solution exists today that meets these workers where they are — in a school hallway, a community center, a WhatsApp thread — and provides intelligent, structured, real-time support.

The Solution: Ukubona Digital Twin

Ukubona LLC has built a computational decision-support infrastructure originally designed for national planning at scale. Its core architecture — a recursive pentadic engine represented by the chain T→W→v→Eig→κ (Tensor, Weight, Vector, Eigenstructure, Scalar) — allows any complex, multi-dimensional decision problem to be compressed into structured, actionable outputs.

We are deploying this infrastructure as a direct-to-worker LLM interface: an application that nonclinical mental health workers can access on their phone or laptop to receive structured, intelligent, scenario-specific guidance in real time. The worker describes what they are facing. The system applies structured decision logic. The worker receives clear, grounded, step-by-step guidance — not generic advice, but contextually calibrated support.

The Technical Architecture (NPA-05)

The Ukubona decision-support system is not a thin wrapper around a generic language model. It is built on a computational architecture originally developed for Uganda's National Planning Authority to model national development performance across 146 districts, 100 indicators, and multiple time periods. That architecture — the invariant pentadic chain — is the same logic we apply to decision support in nonclinical mental health work.

The Invariant Chain: T → W → v → Eig → κ

Node Symbol In NPA National Planning In Mental Health Decision Support
① T 146 districts × 100 indicators × time Worker context × youth scenario dimensions × session history
② W Weight matrix: board's explicit national priorities Weight matrix: evidence-based intervention logic (crisis, support, referral)
③ v Dropdown: which PIAP programme is active Query: what scenario is the worker navigating right now?
④ Eig Dominant district clusters surface from data Scenario archetype surfaces: crisis · behavioral · systemic · relational
⑤ κ One national performance number, owned by Government One structured guidance output, grounded in evidence and context

LLM Decision-Support Interface

The frontend is a lightweight, mobile-first web application. Workers interact via natural language chat. The backend routes their input through the pentadic compressor, identifies the relevant scenario archetype, retrieves evidence-based guidance from a curated knowledge base, and generates a structured response via the Claude API.

Critically, the system is designed to augment — not replace — human judgment. Every output includes a confidence indicator, a recommended next human step, and a clear escalation pathway if the scenario exceeds nonclinical scope. The system does not make clinical decisions. It provides the structured scaffolding that helps a peer supporter make better, more consistent, better-documented decisions.

The LLM layer is auditable. Every interaction can be reviewed by supervisors. Every guidance output references the evidence base it drew from. The weights — the intervention logic — are visible, adjustable, and owned by the deploying organization.
